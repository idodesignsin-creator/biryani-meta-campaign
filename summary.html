Affordable CPC is contribution margin × conversion rate. After Amazon's referral fee and FBA, a ₹199 pack supports roughly ₹4–9 per click depending on COGS and conversion. Breaking even at the actual ₹16.28 would need 21–35% conversion. The head term garam masala whole, at its suggested bid of ₹49.61, would need 58%.

This corrects earlier advice to raise bids toward Amazon's suggestion. A suggested bid is what it costs to win the auction, not what the product can afford. The fix is changing which keywords get bought, not paying more for the same ones.

The search terms report settled one question 4 Jun – 8 Aug · 20 terms · ₹576.94

The open worry was that those clicks went to irrelevant queries. They did not. 74% of spend — ₹425.81 — went to people typing what the product literally is: biryani spices, khada masala sabut, biryani whole spices, sabut garam masala. They clicked and did not buy.

So the chain reads: right search → good ad → click → land on the page → leave. That puts the detail page in front. A healthy 10% listing is now effectively ruled out. But 5% conversion remains plausible across 26 clicks, so this points hard without yet proving it.

Three problems, in the order they bind

01 Cost per click exceeds what the price point supports

₹16.28 actual against a ₹4–9 ceiling. No amount of targeting or creative work closes a 2× gap. Either buy cheaper long-tail clicks, or raise average order value — the 180 g ₹299 pack and bundles both lift the ceiling proportionally.

02 A premium price with no on-page justification

₹199 is 2.5–3× mass-market powder. That is defensible, but the page carries 3 reviews and no A+ content. A shopper comparing against a ₹70 packet sees a higher price and less proof. Brand Registry unlocks both A+ content and Vine.

03 The listing title appears to have dropped "biryani"

The reported title no longer contains biryani, khada garam masala or 50+ servings. If real, that costs organic search on the exact term the whole product is built around — and removes the on-listing source substantiating the servings claim. Verify against the live page.

At ₹8–9 per click that is 13–15 clicks a day, 150 clicks in 10–11 days for about ₹1,300. Zero sales at 150 clicks convicts the listing outright — stop spending and fix the page. Any sales, measure the rate and recompute.

This campaign will not be profitable, and that is fine. Break-even at ₹8 per click needs 10–17% conversion depending on COGS. The ₹1,300 is the price of an answer, not an investment expected to pay back. A 5–8% result would be good news: it means the listing works and the only problem is that ₹199 cannot carry ₹8 clicks — fixed with the 180 g pack, bundles or price, not with ad tweaks.
