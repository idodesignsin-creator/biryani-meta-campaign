A new ASIN, written with the rules this project already uses (see `listing-rewrite.md`): 75-character
title cap, 125-character Item Highlight, 250-byte backend, and **no repeated words across
title + highlight + backend**, since Amazon indexes each word once.

Before the copy, here is a fact-check of the existing 200 g listing text. Several claims in it

## 1. Fact-check of the supplied details

### Remove: health claims

| Claim in the 200 g copy | Problem |
| --- | --- |
| "Natural weight management", "supports metabolism" | These are health claims. The FSSAI Advertising & Claims Regulations 2018 do not allow them on a food without substantiation. The evidence behind them is weak: trials of HCA show small, inconsistent weight effects. |
| "Natural detox drink" | "Detox" is an unsubstantiated claim, and it also turns the product into a drink/supplement. |
| "Reducing blood sugar", "anti-ulcer", "favoured by people with gastric sensitivities" | These are disease or medicinal claims. They are the riskiest kind for a grocery listing. |
| "Rich in HCA" as a selling point | Kudampuli rinds do contain HCA. But in this context it reads as a weight-loss claim. Separately, concentrated *Garcinia cambogia* supplements have been linked to liver-injury case reports. That is a reason not to invite supplement-style use. |

There is also a commercial reason. Weight-loss wording pulls in "garcinia cambogia pills"
shoppers. They do not want dried rinds for fish curry, so they click without buying, and some
return or review badly. The buyer who converts is the **cook**. The 200 g campaign's 8% conversion
came from cooking-intent terms.

### Correct

| Supplied | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| "Meen Veveichathu" | **Meen Vevichathu** (the Kottayam-style red fish curry) |
| Scientific name "*Garcinia cambogia*" | Accepted name **_Garcinia gummi-gutta_**. *G. cambogia* is the older synonym, and it is fine to mention as "also known as". |
| "Seedless… no seeds or grit" | "No grit" is an absolute promise that one bad batch breaks. Use **"deseeded, hand-sorted"**. Keep "sorted" only if it really happens. |
| "Preserves the rinds for years" | Promise only what the printed **best-before** says. |

### Verify before using (unconfirmed, same standard as the biryani listing)

| Claim | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| **Kuttanadan origin** | That the 450 g stock really comes from Kuttanad / Alappuzha. Also check what the **200 g** product actually is: its description is headed "What is Kuttanadan Kudampuli?". If the 200 g is not from Kuttanad, that heading is mislabelled. |
| "Export Grade" | This was flagged on the biryani listing too (`title-revision-check.md`). Keep it only if there is a grade or spec to back it up. |
| **"No additives"** | Some traditional processors rub **salt and coconut oil** into the rinds while curing. If yours does, the ingredients must say so, and "no additives" cannot be used. |
| "Airtight Spiceto jar" | Is the 450 g pack a jar, or a zip pouch? Bullet 5 below depends on the answer. |
| **Kuttanad vs Idukki comparison** (fruit 70–100 g, "sharper acidity", "rounded tang") | These are unsourced folk distinctions and the fruit-weight figure cannot be verified. **Keep them out of the listing.** They also create a problem: the biryani product sells "Idukki" as a mark of quality, and a comparison that ranks one region over the other undercuts one of your own products. Sell Kuttanad on provenance, not by comparison. |

### Correct as written

These are fine to keep: wood-smoked and sun-dried rinds turning dark brown or black; Malabar
tamarind, pot tamarind (*kudam* = pot) and fish tamarind as names; the key souring agent in Kerala
fish curry; different from regular tamarind (*Tamarindus indica*); pieces hold their shape in the
gravy; rinse, soak 10–15 min, and add with the soaking water; store airtight, cool and dark.

**Do not call it kokum.** Kokum is *Garcinia indica*, a different fruit. Shoppers do mix them up,
but putting "kokum" in the listing would be inaccurate.

## 7. Decision to make before creating the ASIN

**If the 200 g product is also Kuttanadan Kudampuli, list the 450 g as a size variation of it**
instead of as a new standalone ASIN. Variations share reviews and sales history, and the 200 g
is the account's proven converter (8% conversion, ₹8.63 CPC in `sp-optimisation-plan.md`).
A brand-new ASIN with zero reviews converts worse than any copy can fix.

If the two products come from different regions, they must stay separate ASINs. Also fix the
200 g description's "Kuttanadan" heading, and remove its health-claim bullet (§1).

### Specialty

These are safe as long as they match the pack:

| Value | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| `Wood-Smoked` | True for traditional kudampuli |
| `Sun-Dried` | True |
| `Deseeded` | Only if the rinds are deseeded |

FSSAI the claim needs lab testing), `Export Grade` (unverified), or anything about health or
weight loss.
