## Reading it

**The buy box was held 100% of the time.** This rules out the cheap explanations — no
stock-out, no suppression, no competing offer. 119 people reached a fully purchasable page
and 118 left. The failure is persuasion, not availability.

**One order is a small sample, so the rate was tested rather than assumed** (n = 138):

| If true conversion were | P(≤1 order in 138 sessions) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | 0.0008% | ruled out |
| **5%** | **0.70%** | **ruled out** (was only "unlikely" at n=119) |
| 3% | 7.9% | unlikely |
| 2% | 23% | plausible |
| 1% | 60% | plausible |

**95% upper confidence bound: 3.39%.**

That bound is the robust output here. Off a single conversion the point estimate means
little, but the ceiling is solid: **this listing converts below 3.4%.**

Set that against the break-even table below — 3.0% at ₹6 CPLPV, 4.5% at ₹9, 6.0% at ₹12.
The plausible conversion range sits *below* the break-even requirement at every CPLPV except
the most optimistic, where it is a wash at best. This is no longer a risk-appetite judgement;
the arithmetic does not close.

## What it means for the campaign

At ₹500/day and ₹199 per unit. **Ignores COGS, referral fees, FBA and GST** — the generous
version.

| CPLPV | Visits/day | Conversion needed for revenue to *equal* spend | Units/day @ 0.84% | Cost per sale @ 0.84% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹6 | 83 | 3.02% | 0.70 | ₹714 |
| ₹9 | 56 | 4.52% | 0.47 | ₹1,071 |
| ₹12 | 42 | 6.03% | 0.35 | ₹1,429 |

Roughly **3–6% conversion is required just to stop losing money on ad spend alone**, before
the product is paid for. Measured: 0.84%. Even the optimistic 3% only reaches break-even on
revenue at the cheapest plausible CPLPV, and still loses after costs.

**The campaign would spend about ₹1,000 to sell a ₹199 packet.** A 5–10x gap is not
reachable by tuning targeting, creative or bids.

For contrast, at a healthy 10% listing conversion the same ₹500/day produces 4–8 units/day
at a ₹60–120 cost per sale — a real business. The traffic plan is not what's broken.

## Decision: hold the launch

`launch-checklist.md` predicted this before the data existed — *"Meta ads amplify whatever
your Amazon listing already does"*, and *"if the listing has fewer than 10 reviews and no A+
Content, fixing those two things will return more than the first ₹15,000 of Meta spend."*
The baseline confirms it numerically. The listing, not traffic, is the binding constraint.

The campaign artefacts stay ready — targeting, copy and structure are unaffected and remain
valid.

### The unlock is Brand Registry

All three of this project's blockers resolve through one door:

| Blocker | Unlocked by |
| --- | --- |
| Conversion — no A+ Content on a premium-priced spice | Brand Registry |
| Trust — 3 reviews, below the ~10 where cold traffic stops bouncing | Vine (Brand Registry) |
| Blindness — no way to attribute a sale to Meta | Amazon Attribution (Brand Registry) |

### Relaunch gate

Re-pull this exact report after the listing work. **Launch when unit session percentage is
at or above 5%**, measured over 300+ sessions. Below that the economics above do not close.

### Next step: Sponsored Products, not Meta

Organic traffic is too thin to re-measure against in any reasonable time:

| If 138 sessions spanned | Sessions/day | Days to reach 300 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14 days | 9.9 | 30 |
| 30 days | 4.6 | 65 |
| 60 days | 2.3 | 130 |
| 90 days | 1.5 | 196 |

So buy the traffic to test with — but buy it on Amazon, not Meta. `launch-checklist.md`
already suggested a small Sponsored Products budget for seeding reviews; it is also the
correct **diagnostic instrument**, for reasons that go beyond reviews:

- **No Brand Registry required.** Unlike A+ Content, Vine and Attribution, SP can run this
  week. It is the only lever currently unblocked.
- **Amazon reports conversion natively, per campaign.** The attribution blindness that
  undermines the Meta plan does not exist here.
- **Sessions on demand** — re-measure in two weeks instead of two to six months.
- **It bounds conversion from above.** SP traffic is someone typing "biryani masala" into
  Amazon with intent to buy; Meta traffic is someone scrolling Reels who was not shopping.
  **If in-market Amazon searchers will not buy this listing, cold social traffic will not
  either.** Testing on Meta first pays a premium to learn what the cheaper, cleaner
  instrument reports faster.
- **It seeds the reviews** that the conversion problem partly stems from.
- **The search-term report** shows what buyers actually type — which feeds directly into the
  title problem below.

Rough cost: ₹200–300/day for two weeks ≈ ₹4,000, against ₹15,000 for a blind Meta cycle.

2. **Message match.** Ad says "biryani spices", page says "garam masala". That gap costs

3. **Substantiation.** `ad-copy.md`'s compliance table justifies "50+ servings" by citing the

   — which says **100+**, the figure the same table rules out. Re-establish the source before
   that claim runs in an ad.
