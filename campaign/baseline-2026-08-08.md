## Reading it

**The buy box was held 100% of the time.** This rules out the cheap explanations — no
stock-out, no suppression, no competing offer. 119 people reached a fully purchasable page
and 118 left. The failure is persuasion, not availability.

**One order is a small sample, so the rate was tested rather than assumed:**

| If true conversion were | P(≤1 order in 119 sessions) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15% | ~0% | ruled out |
| 10% | 0.005% | ruled out |
| 5% | 1.6% | unlikely |
| 3% | 12% | plausible |
| 2% | 31% | plausible |
| 1% | 67% | plausible |

The exact rate is unknown; that it is **not healthy** is not. True conversion sits somewhere
around 1–3%.

## What it means for the campaign

At ₹500/day and ₹199 per unit. **Ignores COGS, referral fees, FBA and GST** — the generous
version.

| CPLPV | Visits/day | Conversion needed for revenue to *equal* spend | Units/day @ 0.84% | Cost per sale @ 0.84% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹6 | 83 | 3.02% | 0.70 | ₹714 |
| ₹9 | 56 | 4.52% | 0.47 | ₹1,071 |
| ₹12 | 42 | 6.03% | 0.35 | ₹1,429 |

Roughly **3–6% conversion is required just to stop losing money on ad spend alone**, before
the product is paid for. Measured: 0.84%. Even the optimistic 3% only reaches break-even on
revenue at the cheapest plausible CPLPV, and still loses after costs.

**The campaign would spend about ₹1,000 to sell a ₹199 packet.** A 5–10x gap is not
reachable by tuning targeting, creative or bids.

For contrast, at a healthy 10% listing conversion the same ₹500/day produces 4–8 units/day
at a ₹60–120 cost per sale — a real business. The traffic plan is not what's broken.

## Decision: hold the launch

`launch-checklist.md` predicted this before the data existed — *"Meta ads amplify whatever
your Amazon listing already does"*, and *"if the listing has fewer than 10 reviews and no A+
Content, fixing those two things will return more than the first ₹15,000 of Meta spend."*
The baseline confirms it numerically. The listing, not traffic, is the binding constraint.

The campaign artefacts stay ready — targeting, copy and structure are unaffected and remain
valid.

### The unlock is Brand Registry

All three of this project's blockers resolve through one door:

| Blocker | Unlocked by |
| --- | --- |
| Conversion — no A+ Content on a premium-priced spice | Brand Registry |
| Trust — 3 reviews, below the ~10 where cold traffic stops bouncing | Vine (Brand Registry) |
| Blindness — no way to attribute a sale to Meta | Amazon Attribution (Brand Registry) |

### Relaunch gate

Re-pull this exact report after the listing work. **Launch when unit session percentage is
at or above 5%**, measured over enough sessions to be meaningful (target 300+ sessions, not
119). Below that the economics above do not close.

## Separate finding — the title appears to have changed

| Source | Title |
| --- | --- |
| Spec, recorded 2026-08-06 | Spiceto Premium Whole Garam Masala **- Biryani Whole Spices, Khada Garam Masala** |
| This report, 2026-08-08 | Spiceto Premium Whole Garam Masala **90g \| 12 Hand-Weighed Spices** |

`biryani`, `khada garam masala` and `50+ servings` are all absent from the current title.
**Verify against the live listing** — this may be a report-field artefact, but if real:

1. **Organic search.** The whole campaign, creative and copy are built on *biryani*, and the
   listing no longer carries the term. Plausibly part of why sessions are this low.
2. **Message match.** Ad says "biryani spices", page says "garam masala". That gap costs
   conversion on precisely the traffic the campaign would buy.
3. **Substantiation.** `ad-copy.md`'s compliance table justifies "50+ servings" by citing the
   title. If the title no longer states it, the only on-listing source left is the A+ content
   — which says **100+**, the figure the same table rules out. Re-establish the source before
   that claim runs in an ad.
